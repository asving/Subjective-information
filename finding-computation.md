# Finding computation in physical processes

*Notes toward an observer-dependent theory of computation. Summarizes a line of discussion between Asvin G. and Claude (Fable 5), July 2026, surrounding the draft `epiplexity-reformulation.tex` in this repository. The technical results referenced here are proved in that draft; these notes supply the motivation, the interpretation, and the open problems.*

## 1. The question

When does a physical system implement a computation?

The question has a famous pathology. Putnam and Searle argued that under an unconstrained choice of encoding, every sufficiently complex physical system implements every finite computation: a rock implements WordStar, a waterfall plays chess. The mappings that witness these claims are gerrymandered, but classically there is nothing to rule them out, because classical accounts place no cost on the mapping. Every attribution of computation to matter is mediated by a map from physical states to computational states, and if maps are free, attributions are trivial.

Aaronson's response points in the right direction: require the encoding and decoding maps to be computationally cheap relative to the computation being attributed, so that the credit for the work goes to the system rather than to the map. We take this as a pointer rather than an answer, for three reasons. First, "cheap" is defined relative to a reference machine, and for finite token systems (this rock, this neural network, now) the machine-dependent constants can dominate; the invariance theorems that wash them out are asymptotic. Second, the criterion is naturally graded while the question was posed as binary, and nothing fixes the threshold. Third, nothing anchors the choice of resource measure itself.

The reframing we adopt instead: do not begin with an abstract computation and ask which physical systems implement it. Begin with physical processes, treat computation *as* a class of physical processes (this is the direction of fit in the companion essay *Computational Platonism*), and ask when two processes count as the same computation, for whom, and at what price. The triviality argument presupposes the abstract-first direction; run the definition the other way and the problem becomes one of pricing an equivalence relation, which is a mathematical question.

## 2. Observers as priced program classes

The first requirement is a definition of the observer, since "observer-dependent" is empty until the observer is a mathematical object. The definition that makes everything downstream work is deflationary: an **observer** is a pair (O, K), a class of programs together with a cost function on them. No minds, no perspectives. Subjectivity enters only through which programs you can run and what they cost you.

Given a prediction problem p (predict outcome y from input x, both generated by the physical process under study), the observer's best account of it is the **subjective entropy**

    M^λ_O(p) = min over u in O of { λ·K(u) + H(p ‖ u) }

where H(p ‖ u) is cross-entropy and λ is an amortization parameter: K(u) is bits, H is bits per sample, and λ = 1/n is the exchange rate set by how many samples the program will be used on. At the optimum the value splits into structural information S (the program) and residual entropy H (what remains noise *to this observer*).

Two prior notions are the two axes of this definition. Predictive V-information (Xu, Zhao, Song, Stewart, Ermon 2020) restricts the program class but prices nothing (K ≡ 0). Epiplexity (Finzi, Qiu, Jiang, Izmailov, Kolter, Wilson 2025) restricts by time bound and prices by program length. Shannon entropy restricts nothing and prices nothing. The draft develops the common generalization and its calculus.

## 3. What the formalism delivers

**A calculus, from one mechanism.** The classical entropy inequalities (subadditivity, chain rule, Shearer) survive for bounded observers, and the draft's master theorem explains why in one sentence: cross-entropy is linear in the logarithm of the program, so any log-linear method of assembling programs decomposes exactly, with defect equal to the expected log-normalizer. Every inequality of Shannon type then splits into a computational part (the observer's closure under the assembly, priced in K) and an analytic part (a bound on the normalizer). For the Shannon observer the computational part is free and the analytic part is convexity; for bounded observers the computational part carries all the content.

**A bounded data-processing inequality.** Classically, deterministic processing cannot create information. For bounded observers it can (decrypting a ciphertext creates usable information), and this failure is the founding observation of both V-information and epiplexity. The bounded DPI locates and prices it:

    I_O(φ(X) → Y)  ≤  I_O(X → Y) + K_O(φ).

Processing creates usable information only relative to observers who cannot afford the processing, and the amount created is at most the observer's price for it. As a corollary, cheaply invertible processing changes nothing: subjective entropy is invariant under cheap re-parameterizations of the input. Cheap invertible maps are the gauge group of the theory, and M_O is a gauge invariant.

**A frontier instead of a number.** Sweeping λ traces the Pareto frontier between structural investment and residual noise. The optimizer walks down it as the amortization horizon grows, acquiring structures in increasing order of their price-to-benefit ratio ΔK/ΔH; discrete structure makes the walk jump, so capabilities appear suddenly at predictable data scales.

## 4. The payoff for the original question

**The triviality argument becomes a theorem about unpriced maps.** Define the **implementation surplus** of a reading φ of a physical system: the predictive compression the reading delivers, minus the observer's price K_O(φ) for the reading. The bounded DPI says the surplus is the only part of an attribution that cannot be manufactured by the map itself. The waterfall implements chess with surplus at most zero: the map costs everything it delivers. A CPU implements arithmetic with enormous surplus: a trivial φ (read these voltages) unlocks vast compression of the system's behavior. "Credit goes to the system, not the map" is no longer a slogan; it is an inequality.

**Implementation is graded and frame-relative, but objective within a frame.** Two observers whose program classes inter-simulate at bounded overhead agree on all subjective entropies up to that overhead. Attributions of computation are relative to a resource frame the way simultaneity is relative to an inertial frame, with complexity classes as the symmetry group. A stronger observer genuinely sees computations in a system that a weaker one cannot; this is not a paradox but the theory working. Cryptography shows the hierarchy is strict: a pseudorandom generator's output has structure visible to no polynomial observer and full structure for an exponential one.

**"Does this system compute?" dissolves into a curve.** The well-posed object is the system's frontier: how much structure each observer class extracts at each horizon. Being a computer means having a steep frontier, in gauges cheap relative to the system's size. A rock's frontier is flat. The question "is it computing?" was malformed the way "is this gas hot?" is malformed; temperature needed the right state function, and so does computation.

## 5. Two laboratories

**Mechanistic interpretability is the experimental science of this relation.** "When does a neural network implement an algorithm?" is the implementation problem posed about a token physical system, where asymptotics are unavailable. The field's working norms are the theory's constraints, rediscovered empirically: probes must be simple, or the probe is doing the computation (the waterfall argument as a methodological rule); explanations are judged partly by description length; linear probing works at all because the network's own layers read the residual stream through (bi)linear operations, so gradient descent reinforces exactly the features that are cheaply readable by the network's internal observers. A sparse autoencoder is a purchased gauge transformation: expensive to find, cheap to apply, permanently lowering the marginal cost of reading. The implicit empirical law beneath the whole field, stated in this framework: stochastic gradient descent does not produce cryptographically obfuscated representations. That is a refutable claim about nature, believed on the evidence of the traces.

**Mathematics is the λ → 0 laboratory.** Theorems, in this accounting, are purchased amortization: a one-time expenditure of K that permanently lowers the cost of reading certain facts, exactly parallel to the SAE. Enriched presentations of a group trade description length against proof-search time (the experiment in the companion `semantic-computation` code measures this trade for presentations of S_n). And Arnold's quip that mathematics is the part of physics where experiments are cheap has a sharp translation: cheap experiments mean effectively unbounded n, hence λ → 0, the infinite-amortization regime where maximal structure is rational. This is why high-cost non-constructive posits (choice, large cardinals) are reasonable investments in mathematics and would not be in a single physics experiment. Separately, the "surprise" that makes mathematics experimental (the central claim of *Computational Platonism*) gets its formal definition here: surprise is subjective-information gain that classical DPI forbids, possible precisely because mathematicians are bounded observers of systems that out-compute them.

## 6. Open problems

1. **Interventions.** Everything above is distributional: M_O sees trace statistics. A genuine implementation claim must commute with intervention; the decoding should predict what the system *would* do under perturbation, not just what it does (in interpretability: patching, not probing, is the gold standard, and a decodable feature can be epiphenomenal). The needed extension indexes the prediction problem by a family of interventions and requires the surplus to survive across the family: a do-calculus for bounded observers. Nothing here yet.

2. **The canonical observer.** The framework makes observer-dependence precise but does not pick out nature's observer; K is still a choice, and the proposal to price it physically (time, energy, thermodynamic work via Landauer) is a promissory note. The conjectured resolution: the canonical observer for a system is *internal* to it. A system's own downstream dynamics is a priced program class, and implementation should be measured relative to that (for a transformer, the internal reads are linear, which is why the linear representation hypothesis is true when it is). Formally this asks for the resource-bounded ε-machine: the minimal O-sufficient statistic, definable through the draft's Chain Rule Equality (a summary map is O-sufficient when the chain rule is tight at negligible overhead). Building this object, and its lattice as O varies, is the main open construction.

3. **Hierarchy.** Real attributions stack: this circuit implements an adder implements arithmetic. Composed decodings should compose surpluses up to overheads, and the master theorem is the natural tool. A chain rule for implementations ought to be a corollary; it has not been written down.

4. **The exchange-rate problem.** A probe costs capacity (its own description), search (compute and samples to fit it), and query time (marginal cost per use). No universal exchange rate reduces these to one currency, and there may be none; the rates may be system-dependent, like temperature. The candidate universal floor is thermodynamic work.

## 7. Status

The honest summary: the implementation problem has been converted from a philosophical puzzle into bookkeeping inside a specific formalism. We have the state functions (M, S, H, I), the inequalities they obey (the master theorem's calculus; the bounded DPI as "no free information from processing, up to the processor's price"), the invariances (cheap gauge transformations), and the conjugate-variable structure (λ dual to K; the frontier as an equation of state). What is missing is the microscopic theory: interventions, the internal observer, and the physical grounding of the cost function. The right analogy for the current state is thermodynamics before statistical mechanics: phenomenological laws in place and provably consistent, mechanism open.
